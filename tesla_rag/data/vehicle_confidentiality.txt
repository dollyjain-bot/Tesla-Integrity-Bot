1.0 EXECUTIVE MANDATE AND OPERATIONAL SCOPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Executive Mandate:
Tesla's competitive leadership depends heavily on continuous technological breakthroughs in electric powertrain efficiency, structural battery architectures, autonomous driving algorithms, custom silicon design, and humanoid robotics. Unauthorized disclosure or leak of unannounced vehicle designs, pre-production software builds, or performance benchmarks severely damages Tesla's brand equity, market positioning, and intellectual property rights.

1.2 Scope:
This Policy applies strictly to all Tesla engineers, software developers, test drivers, prototype assembly technicians, manufacturing operators, executive staff, supplier representatives, and visitors who interact with pre-production vehicle hardware, prototype software builds, or confidential testing facilities globally.

1.3 Non-Real Policy Disclaimer:
THIS DOCUMENT IS A SYNTHETIC DEMONSTRATION ARTIFACT CREATED FOR PORTFOLIO AND ACADEMIC RAG BENCHMARKING PURPOSES. IT DOES NOT REPRESENT ACTUAL INTERNAL POLICIES OR PROPRIETARY DATA OF TESLA, INC.

2.0 DEFINITIONS AND CLASSIFICATION LEVELS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Pre-Production Build:
Any prototype vehicle, structural chassis mule, unreleased powertrain assembly, experimental battery cell format, or alpha/beta software release (including experimental Full Self-Driving builds) that has not been publicly announced or released for commercial consumer distribution.

2.2 Optimus Kinematics Model:
Proprietary neural network models, motion control heuristics, actuator torque curves, balance algorithms, and training telemetry governing the movement of Tesla's humanoid robot (Optimus).

2.3 Robotaxi Prototype:
Cybercab or dedicated autonomous ride-hailing vehicle builds operating without steering wheels, foot pedals, or traditional manual driver controls.

2.4 Tamper-Evident Lens Seal:
A specialized high-adhesion security sticker applied over smartphone and camera lenses prior to entering secure prototype facilities. Removal or puncturing of the seal leaves an indelible residue and invalidates facility security clearance.

2.5 Red-Zone Proximity Enclave:
Geofenced engineering bays, prototype design studios, and proving ground tracks where unreleased vehicle software or hardware is actively assembled, operated, or benchmarked.

3.0 PRE-PRODUCTION HARDWARE AND SOFTWARE PROTECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Section 3.1 - Vehicle Build Secrecy Protocols (Section 4.2 Mandate):
Employees testing unannounced vehicle software builds, robotaxi prototypes, or next-generation humanoid movement models (Optimus) are strictly bound by non-disclosure agreements (specifically Form TSLA-NDA-V2).
(a) Media Sharing Ban: No photographs, telemetry graphs, performance benchmark figures, CAD wireframes, or internal torque logs may be shared on social media platforms (X/Twitter, LinkedIn, YouTube, Reddit), public code repositories (GitHub, GitLab), or personal messaging apps (WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram).
(b) Prohibition of Informal Discussions: Discussing unreleased vehicle specs or testing results with friends, family members, journalists, or financial analysts is strictly prohibited under all circumstances.
(c) Mandatory NDA Agreement: Access to pre-production builds requires an executed TSLA-NDA-V2 on file in the Tesla Compliance Portal.

Section 3.2 - Humanoid Robotics (Optimus) Kinematics & Movement Model Safeguards:
Movement models, joint actuation algorithms, and neural network weights for Optimus humanoid robots are classified as TOP SECRET - CLASS A INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY.
(a) Air-Gapped Testing: Optimus walking experiments must occur inside windowless, obscured testing enclosures or shrouded proving grounds.
(b) Model Weight Encryption: Neural network weights pushed to Optimus prototype units must be hardware-encrypted using TPM 2.0 security chips, preventing memory extraction if a prototype unit is damaged during physical testing.

Section 3.3 - Robotaxi & Autonomous Fleet Prototype Secrecy:
Autonomous robotaxi prototypes undergoing public road testing under regulatory test permits must feature camouflaged exterior vinyl wrapping and covered interior dashboard consoles when parked in public spaces. Test drivers must deploy optical window covers immediately if a prototype vehicle breaks down on public roadways.

4.0 PROVING GROUNDS AND PROTOTYPING BAY PHYSICAL SECURITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Section 4.1 - Test Track Security Protocols (Section 4.3 Mandate):
Personal mobile devices, smartwatches with camera capabilities, bodycams, and unauthorized recording equipment are strictly forbidden inside Gigafactory prototype assembly bays, design centers, and private proving grounds (e.g., Fremont Track, Texas Proving Ground).
(a) Lens Sealing Obligation: All camera lenses on approved mobile devices entering secure facilities must be sealed with tamper-evident security tape by security personnel at entry checkpoints.
(b) Confiscation Authority: Security guards possess full authority to inspect smartphone lenses upon exit. Broken or tampered security tape results in immediate device confiscation and forensic security audit.

Section 4.2 - Red-Zone Proximity Badging and Access Control:
Access to Red-Zone enclosures is restricted to personnel holding active Red-Zone security badges. Badges must be visibly displayed at chest height at all times. Tailgating behind authorized personnel through secure turnstiles is cause for immediate security citation.

5.0 DATA AND TELEMETRY EXPORT CONTROLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Section 5.1 - CAN-Bus Data Isolation and Torque Graph Security:
Telemetry recorded during prototype vehicle road testing (CAN-bus logs, motor inverter temperatures, acceleration telemetry) must be ingested directly into encrypted engineering servers via secure wireless telemetry relays. Copying raw CAN-bus log files to unencrypted USB flash drives or personal laptops is strictly forbidden.

Section 5.2 - Endpoint Data Loss Prevention (DLP):
All engineering workstations assigned to vehicle testing teams are monitored by automated Endpoint DLP agents. DLP agents automatically block file transfers containing vehicle telemetry extensions (`.tlog`, `.canlog`, `.optimusbin`) to cloud storage drives, external email accounts, or unauthorized external domain addresses.

6.0 VENDOR AND PARTNER NON-DISCLOSURE MANAGEMENT (TSLA-NDA-V2)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 Third-Party Binding Requirements:
Prior to providing external suppliers, tooling vendors, or battery cell partners with CAD files or prototype build specifications, the supplier must execute the TSLA-NDA-V2 Master Security Agreement.

6.2 Supplier Facility Inspection:
Tesla Corporate Security reserves the right to conduct unannounced physical and cybersecurity audits of supplier manufacturing plants to verify that prototype tooling molds and stamping dies for unreleased vehicles are stored in locked, access-controlled rooms.

7.0 EMPLOYEE RESPONSIBILITIES AND VISITOR ESCORT PROTOCOLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1 Visitor Escort Requirement:
All non-cleared visitors, external vendors, and maintenance technicians entering prototype facilities must be continuously escorted by a Red-Badge employee at a ratio not exceeding 1 escort per 3 visitors.

7.2 Reporting Unidentified Persons:
Employees who observe unbadged individuals or unauthorized photography in prototype bays must immediately challenge the individual and alert Factory Security at extension 2222.

8.0 PERMISSIBLE EXCEPTIONS AND EXECUTIVE SECURITY WAIVERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.1 Written Executive Security Waiver:
Exemptions to photography restrictions or public demonstration bans for marketing reveals or investor presentations require written authorization signed jointly by:
1. Vice President of Vehicle Engineering;
2. Chief Security Officer (CSO);
3. Vice President of Global Communications.

8.2 Verbal Exemptions Invalid:
Verbal statements by product managers, software leads, or external event hosts claiming photography is allowed are completely invalid without an executed Executive Security Waiver Form TSLA-SEC-W4.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9.1 Incident Escalation Window:
Any suspected leak of prototype telemetry, photograph of an unreleased vehicle, or social media post containing pre-production software benchmark graphs must be reported to corporate security within 2 hours of discovery.

9.2 Forensic Investigation Powers:
Tesla Corporate Security and Legal reserve the right to conduct digital forensic audits of company-issued laptops, phone logs, and internal messaging history when investigating prototype confidentiality leaks.

10.0 ILLUSTRATIVE CASE STUDIES AND SCENARIO WALKTHROUGHS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Case Study 1: Posting FSD Telemetry Graphs on Reddit
Scenario: Alex, a firmware test engineer, runs an experimental FSD build on his company vehicle and records impressive lap times on a private test track. Proud of the result, Alex posts a screenshot of the internal telemetry velocity graph on the `/r/TeslaMotors` subreddit using an anonymous account.
Analysis: Alex has breached Section 3.1 (Prohibition of Telemetry Graphs & Social Media Posts) and Section 5.1. Digital watermarking embedded in the graph allows Corporate Security to trace the log to Alex's test run within 4 hours.
Correct Action: Alex is terminated immediately, stripped of equity vestings, and sued for breach of TSLA-NDA-V2.

Case Study 2: Smartphone Security Tape Tampering
Scenario: During a tour of the Optimus assembly line in Austin, an external electrical contractor removes the tamper-evident tape from his iPhone camera lens to photograph an Optimus joint actuator prototype, intending to show it to a colleague.
Analysis: This violates Section 4.1 (Tamper-Evident Security Seal Procedures) and Section 2.2.
Correct Action: Security personnel confiscate the iPhone, escort the contractor off the premises, permanently revoke the contractor's firm from working with Tesla, and initiate legal proceedings.

Case Study 3: Uncovered Breakdown on Public Highway
Scenario: A test driver operating an unreleased Robotaxi prototype experiences a high-voltage battery failure on Highway 101. The driver exits the vehicle to await a tow truck, leaving the transparent glass windows completely uncovered. Passersby take photos of the interior dashboard, revealing the absence of a steering wheel column.
Analysis: The driver failed to follow Section 3.3 (Deployment of Window Covers During Public Breakdowns).
Correct Action: The test driver receives a severe formal reprimand and is required to undergo mandatory retraining on prototype emergency breakdown procedures.
